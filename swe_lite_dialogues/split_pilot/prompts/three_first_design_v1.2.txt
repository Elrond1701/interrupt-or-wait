THREE-FIRST DESIGN POLICY (applies before the extraction/adaptation instructions).
Your default target is one U0/U1/U2 conversation ending at the exact original
task. First establish the complete actual endpoint, then design its updates
within this SAME extraction call. Prefer three, but never add a requirement
just to obtain it. Return requirements and clarification targets before the
assessment and length_design so the design can reference the actual final IDs.
Do not stop with one generic question without considering both routes.
This is synthetic conversation construction, not reconstruction of issue history.

Return length_design as a small, checkable design record:
- route: separate_scopes, clarify_then_revise, or two_message_fallback.
- u1_adds / u2_adds: IDs of the requirements introduced by each update, including
  answers to open questions. Use final r IDs, or t1 for an earlier preference.
  These are proposed updates, not executable implementation steps.
- scope_route_reason: identify independently requestable source scopes, or give
  the concrete reason that splitting the original scope would duplicate, omit,
  or invent behavior. One fix / one bug / could be bundled is not a reason.
- revision_route_reason: identify a plausible earlier user policy and why it
  would change, or explain why no reasonable alternative exists in this case.

Route A: use distinct source scopes and two genuine updates. For two final
requirements, normally leave both questions open in U0 and answer one per turn.
For three scopes, U0 may request one and U1/U2 add the others. Do not repackage
a rule and its direct consequence as independent requests. If final atoms are
LOCKED by the following instructions, do not split those atoms in this mode.

Distinguish separate requested behaviors from extra evidence for the SAME output.
If the issue converts an object to a string merely to reveal whitespace already
reported in its interactive display, those are two ways to inspect one textual
representation, not automatically two independent requirements. Likewise, a
screenshot and a printed value that demonstrate one defect are not extra scopes.
Separate interfaces count only when the source requests separately meaningful
behaviors, not solely because two spellings or viewing methods are present.

Route B: U0 leaves a relevant choice open; U1 chooses a usable temporary policy
t1; U2 explicitly replaces it with the original final requirement(s). The early
preference is deliberately SYNTHETIC: it need NOT appear in the source issue.
Its absence from the issue, or the issue author's already knowing the answer,
is NOT grounds to reject this route. A fail-fast validation policy versus
skipping incomplete observations, or selective versus comprehensive visibility,
can be real product choices. Evaluate suitability for this actual task; do not
copy an example indiscriminately. Explain the user motivation for the temporary
choice and its later change. Give c1 the same target IDs as t1.replaced_by;
u1_adds=[t1], u2_adds=t1.replaced_by. Fully retire t1 at the end.

No manufactured bug: accidental crashes, false mathematics, mangled identifiers,
or simply retaining the reported defect are not reasonable temporary policies.
Do not invent new public options or unrelated features. A temporary policy must
specify observable behavior; inspect / implement / test, restatements, and a
last-turn recap are not substantive updates. Questions must be neutral, and U0
must not disclose the future answers. Keep all original final requirements.

QUALITY CHECK BEFORE COMMITTING TO A LENGTH:
- Source statements that something ALREADY WORKS are regression context, not
  delayed new requirements. A working input form or a mathematically correct
  diagonal sum must not become an artificial open question or extra update.
  Preserve working behavior under the invariant instead. Distinct named APIs
  that actually need the requested change can still be separate scopes even
  when their policy is uniform.
- Two literal strings, numbers, sizes, or demonstrations of the same condition
  do not automatically define separate scopes. Extract their GENERAL requested
  behavior; do not narrow a general bug to those example constants. A component
  name alone is not evidence that all other, already-working cases are new scope.
- An agreement requirement such as A(x) == B(x) is ONE relation, not two
  independently settleable output requirements. Neither endpoint alone can
  resolve half of that same relation.
- Distinguish the definite bug-fix request from optional ideas (e.g. 'could also
  add help text'). Do not promote an optional suggestion into a mandatory final
  feature. Explain the choice briefly; defer if the endpoint remains ambiguous.
  Likewise, a suggested implementation is not automatically a required literal
  implementation. Preserve requested observable behavior.
- Recheck length_design against the actual requirements/temporary_requirements/
  clarifications you have now written. Never refer to an absent r2, assign two
  questions to the same final atom, or keep route=separate_scopes after deciding
  three_messages=not_natural. If only one useful update survives, use the
  two-message fallback and explain both routes. On decision=defer, requirements,
  temporary_requirements, clarifications, u1_adds and u2_adds MUST all be empty.
- Public context and messages must be self-contained: do not refer to 'the
  reproduced plot', 'this matrix', or 'the reported template' without supplying
  the relevant setup, or simply name the component/problem without such a
  reference. Do not leak future policy answers to provide that context.

Fallback: only after both routes lack a meaningful second update, set
route=two_message_fallback, u1_adds to the final IDs settled in U1, u2_adds=[],
and three_messages=not_natural. Give task-specific reasons for BOTH routes;
"no preference in source" is insufficient. Two messages are valid output,
not failure. If deferring the task itself, use this route and empty update IDs.
For a three-message design, set three_messages=natural and actually provide
the distinct scopes/questions or t1 that make both updates possible. Structural
checks do not establish naturalness: semantic review can still reject a design.
